# ADR-025.2 Phase C — Governed-role authorization

## Status

Implemented and qualified on 2026-08-04.

## Runtime delivered

The authenticated-map workflow now evaluates `governed-role` evidence against
the candidate-height domain-actor and direct-policy views. It verifies the
complete action commitment, chain and genesis identities, current immutable
policy and actor revisions, active organization, required role, active key,
claimed public key, authorization lifetime, and strict Ed25519 signature.
Canonical and bound checks precede stateless admission verification; apply
repeats signature verification and owns the authoritative stateful verdict.

Successful execution writes the map batch, receipt, and immutable direct
consumption records under `(actorId, authorizationId)` in one staged component
transition. A failed precondition, value validator, authorization, or replay
writes no entry or consumption. The actor namespace permits two actors to use
the same caller-chosen authorization ID without collision.

All actor-signature paths share the genesis-bound 512-unit block counter.
Deadline height is exclusive: `issuedHeight <= executionHeight <
deadlineHeight`.

## Actor-authenticated governance

Governed authenticated-map profiles now use stable administrator actor IDs
rather than member keys for organization, actor, key, role, direct-policy,
approval-policy, and administrator-authority changes. Canonical
`PROPOSE -> APPROVE -> ACTIVATE` records retain:

- the exact mutation and digest;
- the frozen authority revision and digest;
- not-before and expiry heights;
- proposer identity;
- accepted actor-signed votes and organization snapshots; and
- terminal status, including cancellation, expiry, failure, and supersession.

Any current member can relay canonical actor-signed governance bytes; relay
identity creates no business vote. The proposer may cancel, while other
cancellations require the current distinct-actor threshold. A successor
authority must be approved under its predecessor and atomically supersedes
bounded pending work from the old authority in both actor and policy state.

Registry mutations fail closed if they would leave fewer usable current
administrator actors than the current threshold. This covers actor or
organization suspension, revocation, reassignment, and key rotation. Genesis
records and all later revisions remain immutable under stable current
pointers.

## Query and contract surface

Governed profiles expose bounded exact component queries for current and
historical organizations, actors, administrator authorities, direct and
approval policies, and retained governance mutations. The map component
exposes exact actor-scoped consumption lookup. These records use the Phase-A
physical namespaces and are therefore addressable by the generic composite
proof surface; Phase E owns assembled multi-record proof bundles and domain
HTTP projections.

The role-workflow CDDL now includes administrator votes, retained governed
mutation records, terminal statuses, and the bounded pending index. Policy
mutation decoding also rejects non-canonical encodings.

## Qualification evidence

Focused contract, processor, and complete composite tests cover threshold
approval, proposer cancellation, automatic expiry, authority handover and
supersession, member-neutral relay behavior, policy activation, actor
suspension, unsafe administrator suspension, actor-scoped replay, invalid
signatures, mixed-batch atomicity, exact queries, and same-block stage order.
